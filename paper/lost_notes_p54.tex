\title[The two lost notes on page 54]{The two lost notes on page 54 of Ramanujan's lost notebook}

\author*[1]{\fnm{Otakhon U.} \sur{Kenjaev}}\email{kenjayevotaxon@gmail.com}

\affil*[1]{\orgname{Independent researcher}, \orgaddress{\city{Khorezm}, \country{Uzbekistan}}}

\abstract{On page 54 of his lost notebook Ramanujan states two families of cubic theta-function identities and, in each family, writes ``see note'' next to one further claim ($A^3-B^3$ in the first family, $A^3+B^3$ in the second). Both notes are lost: Andrews and Berndt record that they have not been preserved. We show that both missing identities are special cases of another claim written on the same page, Ramanujan's cubic circular summation (Entry 8.2.3 in Andrews--Berndt), and that in terms of the Borweins' cubic theta function $a(q)=\sum_{m,n}q^{m^2+mn+n^2}$ they take the compact form $A^3-B^3=q^2f^3(-q^3,-q^{12})+f(-q^2,-q^3)\,a(q^5)$ and $q(A^3+B^3)=f^3(-q^6,-q^9)-f(-q,-q^4)\,a(q^5)$. We then prove why a note was unavoidable: unlike the four cube identities Ramanujan recorded, the two deferred combinations vanish in the upper half-plane. The second one vanishes at a real point $q_0\in(-\tfrac{7}{10},-\tfrac12)$ (an intermediate-value argument with exact rational bounds), and the conjugation $u_2(\gamma\tau)\,u_1(\tau)=-1$ by $\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}7&6\\15&13\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_0(15)$, proved with Siegel functions, carries this zero to a zero of $A-B$ in the first family. Since a product of theta functions never vanishes, neither deferred combination---in contrast to every one-term identity on the page---is a theta product in any basis. A finite search shows that, within the basis of page 54, the only two-term product forms are the trivial rewritings $A^3\mp B^3=(A^3\pm B^3)\mp2B^3$ of the recorded one-term identities. All identities are certified to $2000$ coefficients by exact integer arithmetic and independently at 50-digit precision.}

\keywords{Ramanujan's lost notebook, theta functions, cubic theta functions, circular summation, Borwein cubic theta function, Siegel functions}

\section{Introduction}
As usual, let
\[
\f(a,b):=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}a^{n(n+1)/2}\,b^{n(n-1)/2},\qquad |ab|<1,
\]
and $\f(-q):=\f(-q,-q^2)=(q;q)_\infty$. Page 54 of Ramanujan's lost notebook
\cite{LN} contains two groups of cubic identities for theta functions with
quotient of arguments $q^{15}$; they are treated as Entries 8.2.1 and 8.2.2 in
Andrews and Berndt's second volume \cite[pp.~176--178]{AB2}.

\textbf{Entry 8.2.1.} Let $A=\f(-q^7,-q^8)$ and $B=q\f(-q^2,-q^{13})$. Then
\begin{align}
A+B&=\frac{\f(-q^2,-q^3)}{\f(-q,-q^4)}\,\f(-q^5),\tag{8.2.6}\\
A-B&=\f(-q^{2/3},-q)+q^{2/3}\f(-q^3,-q^{12}),\tag{8.2.7}\\
A^3+B^3&=\frac{\f(-q^6,-q^9)}{\f(-q^3,-q^{12})}\,\f^3(-q^5),\tag{8.2.8}\\
(A-B)^3&=\frac{\f(-q^2,-q^3)\,\f^3(-q,-q^4)}{\f(-q^3,-q^{12})}+q^2\,\f^3(-q^3,-q^{12}).\tag{8.2.9}
\end{align}
After stating these, Ramanujan writes ``$A^3-B^3$ \emph{see note}.'' Andrews and
Berndt comment: ``Either Ramanujan never wrote this note, or if he did, it has
not been preserved'' \cite[p.~177]{AB2}.

\textbf{Entry 8.2.2.} Let $A=\f(-q^4,-q^{11})$ and $B=q\f(-q,-q^{14})$. Then
\begin{align}
A-B&=\frac{\f(-q,-q^4)}{\f(-q^2,-q^3)}\,\f(-q^5),\tag{8.2.10}\\
A+B&=-\frac{1}{q^{1/3}}\left\{\f(-q^{1/3},-q^{4/3})-\f(-q^6,-q^9)\right\},\tag{8.2.11}\\
A^3-B^3&=\frac{\f(-q^3,-q^{12})}{\f(-q^6,-q^9)}\,\f^3(-q^5),\tag{8.2.12}\\
(A+B)^3&=-\frac1q\left\{\frac{\f(-q,-q^4)\,\f^3(-q^2,-q^3)}{\f(-q^6,-q^9)}-\f^3(-q^6,-q^9)\right\}.\tag{8.2.13}
\end{align}
Here Ramanujan writes ``$A^3+B^3$ \emph{see note},'' and again ``he apparently
did not write this note, or, if he did, it has been lost'' \cite[p.~178]{AB2}.

To our knowledge the two missing notes have not been reconstructed in the
literature. The cubic identities of page 54 were first proved by Son
\cite{Son}, who already remarks that Ramanujan ``apparently did not write these
notes'' \cite[\S4]{Son}; Andrews and Berndt state that in this section they ``follow
precisely Son's beautiful proofs'' \cite[p.~173]{AB2}, and both ``see note''
remarks, with the statements that the notes are lost, appear inside that very
section. We were unable to find any later source addressing the notes.

This paper has two parts. The first (Section~\ref{sec:circ}) observes that the two
missing identities are hidden two lines further down the same page: they are
special cases of Ramanujan's cubic circular summation, Entry 8.2.3 of
\cite{AB2}, whose right-hand side is the Borweins' cubic theta function
$a(q)$; Section~\ref{sec:rekon} records a second, elementary form obtained from
(8.2.7), (8.2.9), (8.2.11), (8.2.13). The second part (Section~\ref{sec:nol})
explains why Ramanujan could not record these two claims in the one-line product
form of (8.2.8) and (8.2.12): we prove that $A^3+B^3$ of Entry 8.2.2 vanishes at a
real point $q_0\in(-\frac{7}{10},-\frac12)$, that an explicit element of
$\Gamma_0(15)$ conjugates the two families and carries this zero to a zero of
$A-B$ in Entry 8.2.1, and that consequently neither deferred combination is a
product of theta functions in any basis (Theorem~\ref{thm:nol} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:nol}). Section~\ref{sec:yoqlik} adds a finite search showing
that within the basis of page 54 the only two-term product forms are trivial rewritings of the recorded identities, and
Section~\ref{sec:verifier} describes the verification scripts.

\section{The notes as circular summations}\label{sec:circ}
On page 54 Ramanujan also states the following, proved by Son \cite{Son04} and
given as Entry 8.2.3 in \cite[p.~178]{AB2}: for $|ab|<1$ and $q=ab$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circ}
\f^3(ab^2,a^2b)+a\f^3(b,a^3b^2)+b\f^3(a,a^2b^3)
=\f(a,b)\left\{\frac{\f^9(-q)}{\f^3(-q^3)}+27q\,\frac{\f^9(-q^3)}{\f^3(-q)}\right\}^{1/3}.
\end{equation}
With the Borweins' cubic theta functions \cite{BB91,BBG94}
\[
a(q)=\sum_{m,n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{m^2+mn+n^2},\quad
b(q)=\frac{\f^3(-q)}{\f(-q^3)},\quad
c(q)=3q^{1/3}\frac{\f^3(-q^3)}{\f(-q)},
\]
which satisfy $a^3=b^3+c^3$ \cite{BB91}, the brace in \eqref{eq:circ} equals $(b^3(q)+c^3(q))^{1/3}=a(q)$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:3}
Let $A=\f(-q^7,-q^8)$ and $B=q\f(-q^2,-q^{13})$ (Entry 8.2.1). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T3}
A^3-B^3=q^2\f^3(-q^3,-q^{12})+\f(-q^2,-q^3)\,a(q^5).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take $a=-q^3$, $b=-q^2$ in \eqref{eq:circ}, so $ab=q^5$. Then
$\f(ab^2,a^2b)=\f(-q^7,-q^8)=A$,
$a\f^3(b,a^3b^2)=-q^3\f^3(-q^2,-q^{13})=-B^3$,
$b\f^3(a,a^2b^3)=-q^2\f^3(-q^3,-q^{12})$ and $\f(a,b)=\f(-q^2,-q^3)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:4}
Let $A=\f(-q^4,-q^{11})$ and $B=q\f(-q,-q^{14})$ (Entry 8.2.2). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:T4}
A^3+B^3=\frac1q\left\{\f^3(-q^6,-q^9)-\f(-q,-q^4)\,a(q^5)\right\}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take $a=-q^6$, $b=-q^{-1}$ in \eqref{eq:circ}, so again $ab=q^5$. Then
$\f(ab^2,a^2b)=\f(-q^4,-q^{11})=A$. By $\f(a,b)=a\,\f(a^{-1},a^2b)$
\cite[p.~34]{BerndtIII},
\[
\f(-q^{-1},-q^{16})=-q^{-1}\f(-q,-q^{14}),\qquad \f(-q^{-1},-q^6)=-q^{-1}\f(-q,-q^4);
\]
hence $a\f^3(b,a^3b^2)=-q^6\f^3(-q^{-1},-q^{16})=q^3\f^3(-q,-q^{14})=B^3$,
$b\f^3(a,a^2b^3)=-q^{-1}\f^3(-q^6,-q^9)$ and $\f(a,b)=-q^{-1}\f(-q,-q^4)$.
\end{proof}

Theorems~\ref{thm:3}--\ref{thm:4} have exactly the shape of the recorded
identities (8.2.9) and (8.2.13) --- a cube of a level-15 theta function plus one
further term --- except that the second term contains the binary theta series
$a(q^5)$ rather than a theta product. Since \eqref{eq:circ} is printed on the same
page, immediately after the two ``see note'' remarks, it is natural to read
Theorems~\ref{thm:3}--\ref{thm:4} as the content of the lost notes; this reading
is an interpretation, and we label it as such. Section~\ref{sec:nol} shows that
some non-product factor is in fact unavoidable.

\emph{Literature.} We found no statement of Theorems~\ref{thm:3}--\ref{thm:4}, nor
any link between Entry 8.2.3 and the two ``see note'' remarks, in \cite{Son}, in \cite{AB2}, in
the chapter on circular summation of \cite{AB3}, in \cite{AB4}, in
Chan--Liu--Ng \cite{CLN06}, or in Ge--Luo \cite{GL19}. Son's paper
\cite{Son04} was checked through its zbMATH review (Zbl~1062.11023), which
lists the general theorem and the cases $F_n$, $n=2,3,4,5,7$, and through the account of its proof in \cite[Chapter~9]{AB3}.

\section{An elementary second form}\label{sec:rekon}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:1}
Let $A=\f(-q^7,-q^8)$ and $B=q\f(-q^2,-q^{13})$, as in Entry 8.2.1. Then
\begin{multline}\label{eq:T1}
A^3-B^3=\frac{\f(-q^2,-q^3)\,\f^3(-q,-q^4)}{\f(-q^3,-q^{12})}+q^2\,\f^3(-q^3,-q^{12})\\
+3AB\left\{\f(-q^{2/3},-q)+q^{2/3}\f(-q^3,-q^{12})\right\},
\end{multline}
where, by the Jacobi triple product,
\[
AB=q\,\f(-q^7,-q^8)\,\f(-q^2,-q^{13})
=q\,(q^2,q^7,q^8,q^{13};q^{15})_\infty\,(q^{15};q^{15})_\infty^2 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the polynomial identity $A^3-B^3=(A-B)^3+3AB(A-B)$, the claim is precisely
(8.2.9) plus $3AB$ times (8.2.7), both of which are proved in
\cite[pp.~176--177]{AB2}. The product form of $AB$ is two applications of the
Jacobi triple product $\f(a,b)=(-a;ab)_\infty(-b;ab)_\infty(ab;ab)_\infty$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:2}
Let $A=\f(-q^4,-q^{11})$ and $B=q\f(-q,-q^{14})$, as in Entry 8.2.2. Then
\begin{multline}\label{eq:T2}
A^3+B^3=-\frac1q\left\{\frac{\f(-q,-q^4)\,\f^3(-q^2,-q^3)}{\f(-q^6,-q^9)}-\f^3(-q^6,-q^9)\right\}\\
+\frac{3AB}{q^{1/3}}\left\{\f(-q^{1/3},-q^{4/3})-\f(-q^6,-q^9)\right\},
\end{multline}
where
\[
AB=q\,\f(-q^4,-q^{11})\,\f(-q,-q^{14})
=q\,(q,q^4,q^{11},q^{14};q^{15})_\infty\,(q^{15};q^{15})_\infty^2 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$A^3+B^3=(A+B)^3-3AB(A+B)$; substitute (8.2.13) and (8.2.11), proved in
\cite[p.~178]{AB2}.
\end{proof}

Both right-hand sides were verified coefficient-by-coefficient to $2000$ terms
(Section~\ref{sec:verifier}). We emphasize what is, and what is not, claimed:
the two theorems are elementary consequences of Ramanujan's own four recorded
identities, and in that sense they are certainly results Ramanujan possessed.
Comparing \eqref{eq:T1} with \eqref{eq:T3} gives, as a by-product,
$3AB(A-B)=\f(-q^2,-q^3)\{a(q^5)-\f^3(-q,-q^4)/\f(-q^3,-q^{12})\}$ for the pair of
Entry 8.2.1, and in the same way $3AB(A+B)=q^{-1}\f(-q,-q^4)\{a(q^5)-\f^3(-q^2,-q^3)/\f(-q^6,-q^9)\}$ for the pair of Entry 8.2.2.

\section{Why a note was necessary: the deferred combinations vanish}\label{sec:nol}

Throughout, $\HH$ is the upper half-plane, $q=e^{2\pi i\tau}$, $\tau\in\HH$, and
$q^{r}:=e^{2\pi i r\tau}$ for rational $r$. To separate the two families we write
$A_1=\f(-q^7,-q^8)$, $B_1=q\f(-q^2,-q^{13})$ (Entry 8.2.1) and
$A_2=\f(-q^4,-q^{11})$, $B_2=q\f(-q,-q^{14})$ (Entry 8.2.2), and we put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:u}
u_1:=\frac{B_1}{A_1}=q\,\frac{(q^2,q^{13};q^{15})_\infty}{(q^7,q^8;q^{15})_\infty},\qquad
u_2:=\frac{B_2}{A_2}=q\,\frac{(q,q^{14};q^{15})_\infty}{(q^4,q^{11};q^{15})_\infty},
\end{equation}
the common factor $(q^{15};q^{15})_\infty$ having cancelled by the Jacobi triple product.
By a \emph{theta product} we mean a function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prod}
P(\tau)=c\,q^{s}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\f(\pm q^{a_i},\pm q^{b_i})^{k_i},\qquad
c\in\mathbb{C}^\times,\ s\in\mathbb{Q},\ a_i,b_i\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0},\ k_i\in\mathbb{Z},
\end{equation}
with any choice of signs; every one-term identity on page 54 --- (8.2.6), (8.2.8), (8.2.10), (8.2.12), and the products of Theorems~\ref{thm:1}--\ref{thm:2} --- has this form.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:nonvan}
A theta product \eqref{eq:prod} has no zeros in $\HH$. Moreover, for real
$q\in(-1,0)\cup(0,1)$, with real roots $q^{r}$, it is nonzero, and real when $c$ is real.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By the Jacobi triple product, $\f(\pm q^{a},\pm q^{b})=(\mp q^{a};q^{a+b})_\infty(\mp q^{b};q^{a+b})_\infty(q^{a+b};q^{a+b})_\infty$ is an absolutely convergent product of factors $1\mp q^{r}$ with $r>0$ rational, each of modulus in $(0,2)$ since $|q^{r}|<1$. For real $q$ and real roots each factor lies in $(0,2)$, and $q^{s}$ is real.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The two families are conjugate in $\Gamma_0(15)$}
We use the Siegel functions of Kubert and Lang \cite[Chapter~2]{KL}: for $\mathbf a=(a_1,a_2)\in\mathbb{Q}^2$, $z=a_1\tau+a_2$, $q_z=e^{2\pi iz}$ and $B_2(x)=x^2-x+\frac16$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:siegel}
g_{\mathbf a}(\tau)=-q^{B_2(a_1)/2}\,e^{\pi i a_2(a_1-1)}\,(1-q_z)\prod_{n\ge1}(1-q^nq_z)(1-q^nq_z^{-1}).
\end{equation}
They satisfy, for $\gamma\in SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ and $\mathbf b\in\mathbb{Z}^2$ \cite[Chapter~2, (K1), (K2)]{KL},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:K}
g_{\mathbf a}(\gamma\tau)=\varepsilon(\gamma)\,g_{\mathbf a\gamma}(\tau),\qquad
g_{\mathbf a+\mathbf b}(\tau)=\varepsilon(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)\,g_{\mathbf a}(\tau),\qquad
g_{-\mathbf a}=-g_{\mathbf a},
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon(\gamma)$ is a twelfth root of unity independent of $\mathbf a$ (it comes from $\eta^2$), $\mathbf a\gamma$ is the row-vector action, and
$\varepsilon(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)=(-1)^{b_1b_2+b_1+b_2}e^{-\pi i(b_1a_2-b_2a_1)}$.
In particular a quotient $g_{\mathbf a}/g_{\mathbf b}$ transforms under $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ without any root of unity.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:siegel}
With $g_r:=g_{(r/15,\,0)}(15\tau)$,
\[
u_1(\tau)=\frac{g_2}{g_7},\qquad u_2(\tau)=\frac{g_1}{g_4}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $\mathbf a=(r/15,0)$ and the variable $15\tau$ we have $q_z=q^{r}$, so by \eqref{eq:siegel}
\[
g_r=-q^{\,e(r)}\,(q^{r};q^{15})_\infty(q^{15-r};q^{15})_\infty,\qquad
e(r)=\tfrac{15}{2}B_2(\tfrac{r}{15})=\tfrac{r^2}{30}-\tfrac r2+\tfrac54,
\]
and $e(2)=\frac{23}{60}$, $e(7)=-\frac{37}{60}$, $e(1)=\frac{47}{60}$, $e(4)=-\frac{13}{60}$. Hence
\[
\frac{g_2}{g_7}=q^{\,23/60+37/60}\,\frac{(q^2,q^{13};q^{15})_\infty}{(q^7,q^8;q^{15})_\infty}=u_1,\qquad
\frac{g_1}{g_4}=q^{\,47/60+13/60}\,\frac{(q,q^{14};q^{15})_\infty}{(q^4,q^{11};q^{15})_\infty}=u_2
\]
by \eqref{eq:u}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[conjugation]\label{lem:conj}
Let $\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}7&6\\15&13\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma_0(15)$. Then for all $\tau\in\HH$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conj}
u_2(\gamma\tau)\,u_1(\tau)=-1 .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $\tau'=15\tau$; then $15\,\gamma\tau=\gamma'\tau'$ with $\gamma'=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}7&90\\1&13\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:siegel} and the first relation in \eqref{eq:K},
\[
u_2(\gamma\tau)=\frac{g_{(1/15,0)}(\gamma'\tau')}{g_{(4/15,0)}(\gamma'\tau')}
=\frac{g_{(7/15,\,6)}(\tau')}{g_{(28/15,\,24)}(\tau')}.
\]
Now reduce the indices modulo $\mathbb{Z}^2$ with the second relation in \eqref{eq:K}: $(7/15,6)=(7/15,0)+(0,6)$ gives the factor $\varepsilon=(-1)^{6}e^{\pi i\cdot 6\cdot 7/15}=e^{4\pi i/5}$, and $(28/15,24)=(-2/15,0)+(2,24)$ gives $\varepsilon=(-1)^{74}e^{-\pi i\cdot 24\cdot 2/15}=e^{-16\pi i/5}=e^{-6\pi i/5}$. Using $g_{(-2/15,0)}=-g_{(2/15,0)}$,
\[
u_2(\gamma\tau)=\frac{e^{4\pi i/5}\,g_7}{-e^{-6\pi i/5}\,g_2}=-e^{2\pi i}\,\frac{g_7}{g_2}=-\frac{1}{u_1(\tau)}.
\]
(The identity was also checked numerically at six points with $40$-digit arithmetic, to within $4\cdot10^{-38}$; Section~\ref{sec:verifier}.)
\end{proof}

\subsection{The zeros}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:nol}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] There is a real number $q_0\in(-\frac{7}{10},-\frac12)$ with $B_2(q_0)=-A_2(q_0)$; numerically $q_0=-0.570655687750373\ldots$. Hence $A_2+B_2$, $(A_2+B_2)^3$ and the deferred combination $A_2^3+B_2^3$ of Entry 8.2.2 all vanish at $q_0$, i.e.\ at $\tau_0=\frac12+iy_0\in\HH$, $y_0=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|q_0|=0.089281029042\ldots$.
\item[(ii)] Let $\tau_1=\gamma^{-1}\tau_0=\dfrac{13\tau_0-6}{-15\tau_0+7}$, with $\gamma$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:conj}. Then $A_1(\tau_1)=B_1(\tau_1)$. Hence $A_1-B_1$, $(A_1-B_1)^3$ and the deferred combination $A_1^3-B_1^3$ of Entry 8.2.1 all vanish at $\tau_1$; numerically $\tau_1\equiv 0.117021434026+0.043690294673\,i \pmod 1$, i.e.\ $q=0.563611486667+0.509757510046\,i$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) For real $q\in(-1,0)$ the function $u_2$ of \eqref{eq:u} is a convergent product of positive factors times $q$, hence continuous and real. Write $u_2(q)=P_K(q)\prod_{k>K}r_k(q)$ with
$r_k=(1-q^{15k+1})(1-q^{15k+14})/\bigl((1-q^{15k+4})(1-q^{15k+11})\bigr)$ and $P_K=q\prod_{k\le K}r_k$ an exact rational number for rational $q$. For $|q|^{15(K+1)+1}\le\frac12$ and $k>K$ one has $|\log r_k|\le 8|q|^{15k+1}$ (each of the four factors $1-q^{m}$, $m\ge 15k+1$, satisfies $|\log(1-q^m)|\le 2|q|^{m}$ because $|q|^m\le\frac12$), so $\bigl|\log\prod_{k>K}r_k\bigr|\le S:=8|q|^{15(K+1)+1}/(1-|q|^{15})$. Exact rational arithmetic gives
\begin{align*}
P_4(-\tfrac12)&=-0.79954704982494\ldots,&S&<1.1\cdot10^{-22},\\
P_6(-\tfrac{7}{10})&=-1.51863908249240\ldots,&S&<3.1\cdot10^{-16},
\end{align*}
so $u_2(-\frac12)>-1>u_2(-\frac7{10})$ (the script of Section~\ref{sec:verifier} prints the rigorous enclosures). By the intermediate value theorem there is $q_0\in(-\frac7{10},-\frac12)$ with $u_2(q_0)=-1$, i.e.\ $B_2(q_0)=-A_2(q_0)$, where $A_2(q_0)\ne0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:nonvan}; then $A_2^3+B_2^3=A_2^3(1+u_2^3)=0$.

(ii) By \eqref{eq:conj} with $\tau=\tau_1$, $u_1(\tau_1)=-1/u_2(\gamma\tau_1)=-1/u_2(\tau_0)=1$, i.e.\ $A_1(\tau_1)=B_1(\tau_1)$, and $A_1(\tau_1)\ne0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:nonvan}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:nol}
None of $A_1^3-B_1^3$, $A_1-B_1$, $(A_1-B_1)^3$ (Entry 8.2.1) and $A_2^3+B_2^3$, $A_2+B_2$, $(A_2+B_2)^3$ (Entry 8.2.2) is a theta product \eqref{eq:prod}, for any choice of $c,s,m,a_i,b_i,k_i$ and signs. In particular neither deferred combination has a one-term identity in any basis.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Each of the six functions vanishes at $\tau_0$ or $\tau_1$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:nol}), whereas a theta product does not vanish anywhere in $\HH$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:nonvan}); two holomorphic functions on $\HH$ that agree on a neighbourhood of $0$ in the $q$-plane agree on $\HH$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the dichotomy of page 54]
In each family Ramanujan wrote as one-term products exactly the combinations that do not vanish --- $A_1+B_1$ (8.2.6), $A_1^3+B_1^3$ (8.2.8), $A_2-B_2$ (8.2.10), $A_2^3-B_2^3$ (8.2.12) --- and wrote the vanishing combinations $A_1-B_1$, $(A_1-B_1)^3$, $A_2+B_2$, $(A_2+B_2)^3$ as sums of two terms, (8.2.7), (8.2.9), (8.2.11), (8.2.13). Corollary~\ref{cor:nol} shows that this is not a matter of taste: the second group cannot be written with one term. The two deferred combinations belong to the second group, and Theorems~\ref{thm:3}--\ref{thm:4} give their two-term form; what distinguishes them from (8.2.9) and (8.2.13) is that the second term is not a product but $\f\cdot a(q^5)$, and Section~\ref{sec:yoqlik} shows that within the basis of page 54 the only two-term product forms are the trivial ones, $A^3\mp B^3=(A^3\pm B^3)\mp2B^3$, obtained from the recorded identities (8.2.8) and (8.2.12). The deferral to a note is therefore explained to the extent that a finite search can explain it; a proof that no non-trivial two-term product form exists in general is left open.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The ratios $u_1,u_2$ are modular units of level $15$. A computation like that of Lemma~\ref{lem:conj} (which we have carried out numerically, to $30$ digits) shows how $\Gamma_0(15)$ acts on them according to the residue of the upper-left entry modulo $15$: for $\pm1$ both are invariant, e.g.\ under $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\15&16\end{smallmatrix}\right)$; for $\pm4$ each is inverted, e.g.\ $u_1(\gamma\tau)=1/u_1(\tau)$ for $\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}4&1\\15&4\end{smallmatrix}\right)$; for $\pm2$ and $\pm7$ the two are interchanged up to sign or inversion, e.g.\ $u_2(\gamma\tau)=-u_1(\tau)$ for $\gamma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\15&8\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $u_2(\gamma\tau)=-1/u_1(\tau)$ for the $\gamma$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:conj}. The mirror symmetry
$\f(-q^3,-q^{12})\leftrightarrow\f(-q^6,-q^9)$, $\f(-q,-q^4)\leftrightarrow\f(-q^2,-q^3)$ between the two entries, under which (8.2.8) corresponds to (8.2.12) and (8.2.9) to (8.2.13), is the shadow of this conjugation; in particular the two deferrals are two instances of one phenomenon, and both lost notes are the same circular summation \eqref{eq:circ} with the same modulus $ab=q^5$.
\end{remark}

\section{A finite search for two-term product forms}\label{sec:yoqlik}

Corollary~\ref{cor:nol} rules out one-term forms completely. For two-term forms, which the recorded identities (8.2.9) and (8.2.13) do possess, we report a finite search.

\subsection{Non-periodicity of the quadratic cofactors}
Write $A^3+B^3=(A+B)(A^2-AB+B^2)$ and $A^3-B^3=(A-B)(A^2+AB+B^2)$. A power
series with integer coefficients and constant term $1$ has a unique
representation $\prod_{n\ge1}(1-q^n)^{-c_n}$. If $(c_n)$ is periodic with
period $M$, the series equals $\prod_{a=1}^{M}(q^a;q^M)_\infty^{-c_a}$, a
generalized eta-quotient; when $c_a=c_{M-a}$ for $1\le a<M$ it is a theta product in the sense of
\eqref{eq:prod}, since $(q^a;q^M)_\infty(q^{M-a};q^M)_\infty=\f(-q^a,-q^{M-a})/(q^M;q^M)_\infty$.
Conversely, a theta product \eqref{eq:prod} with integer $a_i,b_i$ and $c=1$, $s=0$ is, by the Jacobi
triple product and $1+x=(1-x^2)/(1-x)$, a finite product of factors $(q^a;q^M)_\infty^{\pm1}$, so its
$(c_n)$ is periodic. Computing $(c_n)$ for $n\le 380$
by the logarithmic-derivative recursion we find, in each entry, a sharp split:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Entry & combination & $(c_n)$\\
\midrule
8.2.1 & $A^2-AB+B^2$ \ (factor of the recorded (8.2.8)) & periodic mod $15$\\
8.2.1 & $A^2+AB+B^2$ \ (factor of the lost $A^3-B^3$) & not periodic\\
8.2.2 & $A^2+AB+B^2$ \ (factor of the recorded (8.2.12)) & periodic mod $15$\\
8.2.2 & $A^2-AB+B^2$ \ (factor of the lost $A^3+B^3$) & not periodic\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
(``Not periodic'' means: no period $\le 60$ holds for $n\le 380$; the periodic cases were checked from $n=1$.) The non-periodic cofactors are $A_1^2(1+u_1+u_1^2)$ and $A_2^2(1-u_2+u_2^2)$, which vanish where $u_1\in\{\omega,\omega^2\}$, resp.\ $u_2\in\{-\omega,-\omega^2\}$, $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$; numerically such points exist (e.g.\ $u_1(\tau)=\omega$ at $\tau\approx0.29434+0.06065\,i$), which is consistent with the table, but we have not proved it, and the table rules out any identity of the shape
$A^3\mp B^3=(\text{linear factor known on p.~54})\times(\text{theta product})$ only within the computed range.

\subsection{Systematic search over theta-monomial bases}
We searched for representations
\[
A^3-B^3=\sum_{i=1}^{r} c_i\, q^{s_i} M_i,\qquad r\le 2,\ c_i\in\{\pm1,\pm2,\pm3\},\ s_i\in\{0,\dots,3\},
\]
where each $M_i$ is a monomial of total degree $3$ with exponents in
$\{-1,1,2,3\}$ in at most three functions drawn from the $12$-function basis
\begin{gather*}
\f(-q,-q^4),\ \f(-q^2,-q^3),\ \f(-q^3,-q^{12}),\ \f(-q^6,-q^9),\ \f(-q^5),\\
\f(-q,-q^{14}),\ \f(-q^2,-q^{13}),\ \f(-q^4,-q^{11}),\ \f(-q^7,-q^8),\ \f(-q),\ \f(-q^3),\ \f(-q^{15})
\end{gather*}
--- every theta function occurring in Entries 8.2.1--8.2.2 together with the
relevant $\eta$-type functions ($2344$ monomials; hash-matching on the first
$80$ coefficients, surviving candidates confirmed on $260$). The search was
repeated in the $q^{1/3}$-lattice (setting $q\mapsto q^3$ and adjoining the
functions $\f(-q^{a/3},-q^{b/3})$ occurring in (8.2.7) and (8.2.11): $20$ basis
functions, $11800$ monomials, shifts $s_i\in\{0,\dots,9\}$), and with the
three-term shape $c_1 q^{s_1}M_1+c_2 q^{s_2}M_2\pm3q^{s}AB\,M_3$ suggested by
Theorems~\ref{thm:1}--\ref{thm:2}.

\emph{Control of the instrument.} Run on the recorded combinations, the same
search re-finds Ramanujan's identities exactly: for $(A-B)^3$ of Entry 8.2.1 it
returns the two-term representation (8.2.9), and in the $q^{1/3}$-lattice it
returns (8.2.7) for $A-B$.

\emph{Result.} For $A^3-B^3$ (Entry 8.2.1) and $A^3+B^3$ (Entry 8.2.2) the
search in the integer basis returns exactly three representations each, all trivial: the definition itself, $f^3(-q^7,-q^8)-q^3f^3(-q^2,-q^{13})$, resp.\ $f^3(-q^4,-q^{11})+q^3f^3(-q,-q^{14})$, and, once the coefficients $\pm2$ are admitted, the two rewritings $A^3\mp B^3=(A^3\pm B^3)\mp2B^3=2A^3-(A^3\pm B^3)$ of the recorded one-term identities (8.2.8) and (8.2.12); each candidate was confirmed on all $260$ coefficients. In the $q^{1/3}$-lattice (trivial forms excluded) and with the three-term shape the search returns nothing. This is
consistent with Theorems~\ref{thm:3}--\ref{thm:4}: the missing identities need
the binary theta series $a(q^5)$, which lies outside the product basis. We state the scope honestly: this is a finite search over an explicit basis and
coefficient range; the one-term case is settled by Corollary~\ref{cor:nol}, the non-trivial two-term case in general is not.

\section{The verification scripts}\label{sec:verifier}
All twelve identities of Sections~\ref{sec:circ}--\ref{sec:rekon} --- (8.2.6)--(8.2.13),
\eqref{eq:T1}--\eqref{eq:T2} and \eqref{eq:T3}--\eqref{eq:T4} --- are certified by one self-contained Python
script \texttt{verifier.py} ($152$ lines, no dependencies). It works in
the variable $y=q^{1/3}$ with exact integer arithmetic; every identity
involving a quotient is verified after cross-multiplication, so no division of
power series occurs. Each identity is checked to $400$ coefficients of $q$ by
default, and the run reported here used $2000$ coefficients ($6003$ powers of
$y$; total runtime about one second). The script prints one MOS/FARQ
(match/mismatch) line per identity and exits nonzero on any mismatch; the
archived run prints $12/12$ MOS. A second, independent script \texttt{sonli\_tekshir.py} evaluates all identities as printed (with division and $q^{1/3}$) at five values of $q$ with 50-digit arithmetic; the largest relative discrepancy is $2\cdot10^{-49}$. A third script \texttt{ildiz.py} checks the foundations independently: it compares every theta series used with its Jacobi triple product (3000 coefficients), verifies the circular summation \eqref{eq:circ} itself as an identity of two-variable integer power series in independent variables $a,b$ (all monomials $a^ib^j$ with $i+j\le150$, where the brace is replaced by $a(ab)$), and checks $a^3=b^3+c^3$ in the division-free form $a(q)^3(q;q)_\infty^3(q^3;q^3)_\infty^3=(q;q)_\infty^{12}+27q(q^3;q^3)_\infty^{12}$ (3000 coefficients). \texttt{ildiz\_gp.gp} re-implements the theta series, Theorems~\ref{thm:3}--\ref{thm:4}, both by-products and the Borwein identity from scratch in PARI/GP (1500 coefficients), independently of the Python code.

The script \texttt{nol\_sertifikat.py} accompanies Section~\ref{sec:nol}. Its Part~A is the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:nol}(i): it computes $P_4(-\frac12)$ and $P_6(-\frac7{10})$ as exact rational numbers (denominators of $97$ and $623$ digits), bounds the tails by the inequality stated in the proof, and prints rigorous enclosures of $u_2(-\frac12)$ and $u_2(-\frac7{10})$ on the two sides of $-1$. Its Part~B is a check, at $40$ digits, of Lemma~\ref{lem:conj} at six points, of the location of $\tau_0$ and $\tau_1$, of $|A_1(\tau_1)-B_1(\tau_1)|<10^{-30}$, and of the non-vanishing of the recorded products (8.2.6), (8.2.8), (8.2.10), (8.2.12) at the same points.
